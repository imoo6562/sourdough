4
Stretch & Fold — Sets 1 & 2
Perform two sets of stretch and folds — 4–5 folds per set, rotating the container each time — spaced 30 minutes apart.
5
Add Inclusions
Scatter cold Roquefort chunks and cooled walnuts evenly over the dough surface. Immediately perform a full set of stretch-and-folds — 4–5 folds rotating around the mass — to begin incorporating. Some Roquefort smearing is unavoidable and fine. Do not overwork. Cover and rest 30 minutes. The dough will feel noticeably slacker after this step — expected.
6
Coil Fold — Set 1
Perform coil folds with wet hands, tucking any protruding inclusions back in as needed. The dough should show some strength despite the fat from the cheese. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
7
Coil Fold — Set 2 & Bulk End
Final coil fold — be gentle, you're building final tension, not redistributing. Cover and allow bulk fermentation to complete. Total bulk from initial mix is typically 4–5 hours at 75–78°F. Look for 50–75% volume increase, a domed surface, and bubbles around the edges. Do not rely solely on time.
8
Divide & Pre-shape
Turn dough onto an unfloured surface. Divide into the desired number of pieces. Pre-shape each into a loose round using a bench scraper. Rest uncovered 20–30 minutes until relaxed and spreading slightly.
9
Final Shape & Cold Proof
Shape each piece — be firm but efficient. The fat in the dough means you have a narrower window before it starts tearing. Place seam-side up in floured bannetons. Refrigerate 10–16 hours, uncovered or loosely covered. Longer cold proof develops more acidity, which plays well against the Roquefort.
10
Bake
Preheat Dutch oven at 500°F for 45–60 minutes. Score confidently at a shallow angle, slightly deeper than usual — fat-enriched dough has less oven spring. Bake covered for 2/3 of the total time, then remove lid and continue for the remaining 1/3 until deep mahogany. Watch the bottom heat — cheese increases browning risk; place a baking sheet on the rack below if your Dutch oven runs hot. Pull at internal temp 208–212°F. Cool on a wire rack minimum 2 hours — the interior needs time to set or the crumb will be gummy around the cheese pockets.
Baker's Notes
If dough feels tight at initial mix, add up to 20g more water at that stage — not after salt addition
Score at a shallower angle and slightly deeper than usual — fat from the cheese reduces oven spring
Cheese inclusions increase browning risk on the bottom — place a baking sheet on the rack below if your Dutch oven runs hot
The 2-hour minimum cool is not optional with cheese inclusions — the crumb will be gummy around the pockets if cut too soon

Method
1
Fermentolyse
Add 100g levain to 350g water and stir to combine. Add all three flours and mix until no dry flour remains. Cover and rest 45–60 minutes at room temperature.
2
Add Salt
Dissolve 10g fine sea salt in 25g reserved water. Pour over the dough and incorporate fully using pinch-and-fold until the saltwater is absorbed and the dough feels cohesive.
3
Bulk Fermentation
Bulk ferment at 75–78°F. Perform 4 sets of stretch and folds, spaced 30 minutes apart. Add inclusions during the 3rd set — fold olives and Gruyère in gently to avoid breaking up the cheese. Rest undisturbed after the final set. Look for 50–75% volume increase. Total bulk: approximately 4–5 hours at 76°F.
4
Divide & Pre-shape
Turn dough onto an unfloured surface. Divide into the desired number of pieces. Pre-shape each into a loose round. Rest uncovered 20–30 minutes.
5
Final Shape & Cold Retard
Shape each piece and place seam-side up in floured bannetons. Place each banneton inside a sealed plastic bag. Refrigerate 10–16 hours.
6
Bake
Preheat Dutch oven at 500°F for 45–60 minutes. Lightly mist the surface of the cold loaf before loading. Score, load, cover, and immediately reduce to 450°F. Bake covered for 2/3 of the total time, then remove lid and continue for the remaining 1/3, or until internal temp reaches 208–212°F. Cool on a wire rack minimum 1 hour before cutting.
Baker's Notes
Thin cracker crust, excellent oven spring — keep bulk conservative given the Tirolese content
Drain olives thoroughly and pat dry; excess brine will weaken the dough structure
Grate Gruyère coarsely — fine grating melts too quickly and can make the crumb gummy
Mist surface before loading; sealed banneton bag essential to prevent rye skin from drying out
